## Corrections and holds

1. **Contradicted — `deep_facts.1.text` and `deep_facts.1.claims.1`: Cervantes's death.** The draft says both deaths carry the date 23 April. The Cervantes birthplace museum dates his death to 22 April 1616 and burial to the following day. The BNE's discussion of the original parish register independently identifies the entry as a burial date, although its full page returned 403 during this run. The Oxford Handbook's introduction likewise distinguishes 22 April from the familiar commemorative date; the indexed extract was readable, but the full publisher page failed. Shakespeare's surviving monument supplies 23 April; the Folger record distinguishes that from the parish burial entry of 25 April. Use the readable museum and Folger evidence for the correction, and retrieve the BNE record or full scholarly text before treating the revised anecdote as fully corroborated. [Cervantes museum](https://museocasanataldecervantes.org/meet-cervantes/), [BNE register discussion](https://www.bne.es/es/blog/blog-bne/el-libro-de-difuntos-de-la-iglesia-de-san-sebastian-la-partida-de-enterramiento-de-cervantes), [Oxford Handbook introduction](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290358048), [Folger archival description](https://shakespearedocumented.folger.edu/resource/document/parish-register-entry-recording-william-shakespeares-burial).

   Proposed replacement: “Shakespeare and Cervantes are often linked with 23 April 1616, but the dates conceal two differences. Cervantes died on 22 April and was buried on 23 April in Spain's Gregorian calendar. Shakespeare's monument records his death as 23 April in England's Julian calendar, equivalent to 3 May Gregorian—eleven days after Cervantes.” Update the accompanying claim, rather than retaining the erroneous death date. The eleven-day interval is a calendar calculation from those dates.

2. **Mechanical failure — `deep_facts.2.text`.** `*An Election Entertainment*` uses single-asterisk italics. The pipeline permits paired `**bold**` and plain text only. Use `An Election Entertainment` or `**An Election Entertainment**`. This finding follows the checked-in text and markup rule; it does not need external historical evidence.

3. **Partially supported — `connections.2.connection`: Julian reform date.** “Introduced ... in 46 BC” conflates Caesar's reform decision with its effective calendar year. The US Naval Observatory dates introduction to 45 BC. Prefer “Caesar's Julian calendar, which took effect in 45 BC, was the system the Gregorian reform corrected.” A 46 BC decision can be retained only with an explicit source and distinction. [USNO calendars](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/calendars).

4. **Unverifiable in this review — `key_moments.0.text` / `.claims.4`: Lilius's precise death year.** The bull establishes Antonio's submission of his brother's proposal, but not a death year. The Vatican Observatory's indexed account says the proposal was presented after Aloysius's death; the full page repeatedly timed out. The exact 1576 date is widely repeated, but a readable primary or modern scholarly basis was not secured here. Do not call it disproved. Safer provisional wording: “Lilius did not live to see the reform; his brother Antonio presented his work.” Verify that narrower statement from a readable scholarly source before approval. [Bull, §5](https://fourmilab.ch/documents/calendar/IG_Latin.html), [Vatican Observatory](https://www.vaticanobservatory.va/en/history/astronomy-calendars-and-religion).

5. **Partially supported — `key_moments.1.text` and `connections.6.connection`: Teresa death/burial anecdote.** Catholic sources differ in their presentation of the death date: the Holy See gives the night of 15 October; the older Catholic Encyclopedia gives 4 October. Neither of these readable passages establishes the next-day burial date. This is a source/calendar clarification gap, not proof the anecdote is false. Prefer “Teresa of Ávila died during the calendar change in October 1582; her feast is observed on 15 October” only after sourcing the feast claim, or retain the fuller anecdote with a documented contemporary burial account and independent scholarly support. Remove “best-known casualty,” which is an unsupported superlative and could imply the reform caused her death. [Holy See biography](https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/audiences/2011/documents/hf_ben-xvi_aud_20110202.html), [Catholic Encyclopedia](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14515b.htm).

6. **Corroboration incomplete — `deep_facts.0.text`: Sweden.** The account is supported by Tøndering's explanation, which reproduces an almanac description and transcribes a separately created marriage record dated 30 February. These underlying documents are independent accounts, but reproducing both on one website does not satisfy the policy's distinct-host test. The almanac image link returned 404; the Riksarkivet record opened only an image-viewer shell. Lund confirms a relevant scholarly chapter exists but the full chapter could not be fetched. Retrieve the original almanac and readable scholarly analysis before approving the anecdote. Keep this as an evidence hold rather than deleting the story because of access failures. [Tøndering's account](https://www.tondering.dk/claus/cal/gregorian.php), [document descriptions/transcription](https://tondering.dk/article/en/calendar/60-30feb.php), [Riksarkivet original record](https://sok.riksarkivet.se/bildvisning/C0070974_00078), [Lars Gislén, scholarly chapter record](https://www.particle-nuclear.lu.se/lars-gislen/publication/a7443deb-d006-4349-bc6f-32d223f7b209).
