## Corrections and evidence gaps

1. **`deep_facts.2.text`: misleading authority, legal mechanism and continuity.** The text says Newton “set the value” and Britain moved to gold “without any law being passed,” which invites the mistaken inference that Newton personally fixed the exchange rate without legal action. Newton advised a reduction; George I imposed the ceiling by a royal proclamation dated 22 December 1717. The proclamation survives in full. Its direction was to reduce an existing 21s 6d market valuation to 21s, rather than introduce an entirely new valuation. [Royal proclamation, MINT00334](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/texts/normalized/MINT00334); [Royal Mint Museum, Newton](https://www.royalmintmuseum.org.uk/journal/people/isaac-newton/).

   The claim that the resulting standard “lasted until the twentieth century” also conceals the major 1797–1821 suspension of gold convertibility. This is a material omission in a purported continuous history. [Bank of England history, Restriction Period](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/about/history).

   Proposed replacement: “In 1717 Newton recommended lowering the guinea’s official valuation. George I’s proclamation fixed its maximum value at 21 shillings. Gold nevertheless remained relatively overvalued, helping silver leave circulation and Britain move towards a de facto gold standard.” The final causal sentence still needs a fetched scholarly monetary-history source before strict policy approval; the first two sentences have direct documentary support. Remove the continuity claim rather than adding a compressed history of subsequent monetary regimes.

2. **`deep_facts.0.text`: unsupported comparison and excessive exclusivity.** “Wrote more on these subjects than on physics” needs explicit comparable corpus counts with a defined denominator. The material consulted demonstrates extensive religious and alchemical writing, not that exact comparison. “The scale of it became clear only after” 1936 also overstates a single discovery point: theological works and earlier access to manuscripts predated that sale. Replace both with “Much of this work remained unpublished. The 1936 sale of his private papers brought a large body of it into new hands.” The auction, Keynes’s purchases, and his famous characterization are documented. [Cambridge University Library exhibition](https://wwwe.lib.cam.ac.uk/CUL/exhibitions/Footprints_of_the_Lion/footprints_lion.html); [Rob Iliffe, Newton’s Religious Life and Work](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/contexts/CNTX00001).

3. **`key_moments.1.text`: overbroad withdrawal from publication.** “He long avoided publishing at all” is too categorical. The Royal Society records his new optical manuscript being read in December 1675, expressly mentioning his wish to avoid disputes. Replace with “The ensuing disputes made Newton reluctant to publish further work on optics.” [Royal Society meeting, 9 December 1675](https://catalogues.royalsociety.org/CalmView/Record.aspx?id=JBO%2F5%2F104&src=CalmView.Catalog). The archive describes an actual manuscript presentation, rather than merely inferring reluctance from his later reputation.

4. **`key_moments.2.text`: retain the historical ellipse answer, avoid making it a universal mathematical rule.** An inverse-square force permits elliptic, parabolic or hyperbolic trajectories depending on conditions. The existing wording is presented as Newton’s historical reply, so it is not a demonstrated factual error; adding “for a bound planetary orbit” would prevent an overgeneral reading. The immediate spoken answer was not independently established here. [George E. Smith, Newton’s Principia](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/newton-principia/); [Andrew Janiak, Newton’s Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/newton-philosophy/).

5. **High-risk anecdotes still lack a complete two-origin evidence package.** The apple account, fish-book financing, Keynes quotation and eye experiments have credible documentation, but this pass has not established two independent original accounts on distinct hosts for every factual assertion. Reprinting Newton’s own report in two places remains one origin. The apple story is already attributed to a named witness, which is stronger than presenting a sudden discovery as unqualified fact; retain that attribution. Do not treat the evidence gaps as contradictions or delete material because a fetch failed.

## Dataset chronology discrepancy

The coordinating offline check reports `loadSubjects()` dates of **1643–1726**. The draft has no explicit birth/death years, so this is a dataset issue rather than a demonstrated prose mismatch. MacTutor gives 4 January 1643 and 31 March 1727 in Gregorian reckoning, explicitly distinguishing the Julian birth date. [MacTutor biography](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Newton/). The Mint Museum independently dates his death to March 1727. A 1726 death label can reflect the English legal year beginning on 25 March; mixing it with Gregorian 1643 is misleading. Investigate the Pantheon death year and normalize the source-year convention upstream; do not alter dates in the Profile merely to agree with 1726.
